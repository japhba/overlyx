\begin{abstract}
This is your own example document. It is a plain \LaTeX{} file in a project of your own: what you see is the document itself, edited in place, and \textsf{Ctrl+R} typesets it as a PDF. Edit it, break it, share it --- or delete the project once you know your way around.
\end{abstract}

\section{Editing text}\label{sec:text}

Write as in any word processor. Every paragraph has a style --- normal text, a heading, a list item, a quote --- chosen in the style box at the left of the toolbar (type to search it) or with the keyboard: \textsf{Ctrl+2}, \textsf{Ctrl+3} and \textsf{Ctrl+4} make headings, and typing ``- '' or ``1. '' at the start of a paragraph begins a list. The outline in the files panel follows the headings; click one to jump there.

\emph{Emphasis} is \textsf{Ctrl+E} (or \textsf{Ctrl+I} for italics), \textbf{bold} \textsf{Ctrl+B}; \texttt{typewriter} and small caps are in the Edit\lyxarrow Text Style menu. Footnotes\footnote{A footnote. Click its label to open or close it; \textsf{Ctrl+Alt+F} inserts one.} sit where they belong in the text, and quotes become ``typographic quotes'' as you type them.
\begin{itemize}
\item This is a bullet list.
\item Press\textsf{ Enter} for the next item, \textsf{Tab} to nest it one level deeper, and\textsf{ Shift+Tab} to get out again.
\end{itemize}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A numbered list counts its items ---
\item and a description list sets its first words in bold, like the shortcuts below.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{description}
\item [{Ctrl+M}] inline formula
\item [{Ctrl+Shift+M}] display formula
\item [{Ctrl+R}] typeset the PDF
\item [{Ctrl+F}] find and replace
\item [{Ctrl+Shift+E}] track changes
\end{description}
Cross-references keep themselves up to date: the mathematics are in Section \ref{sec:math}, the figure is Figure \ref{fig:waves} and the shortcuts are collected in Table \ref{tab:shortcuts}. Insert\lyxarrow Label names the current paragraph; Insert\lyxarrow Cross-reference lists every label of the project.

\section{Mathematics}\label{sec:math}

Formulas are typeset while you write them. \textsf{Ctrl+M} starts an inline formula such as $e^{i\pi}+1=0$ and\textsf{ Ctrl+Shift+M} a displayed one. Inside, type \LaTeX{} as you would anywhere else --- \texttt{\textbackslash frac\{a\}\{b\}}, \texttt{x\textasciicircum\{2\}}, \texttt{\textbackslash alpha} --- and it turns into the formula as you type; \textsf{Tab} completes a command, the arrow keys leave the formula:

\begin{equation}
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-x^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\sqrt{\pi}.\label{eq:gauss}
\end{equation}

While the cursor is in a formula, the math toolbar holds Greek letters, operators, relations, arrows, accents, fonts and delimiters. Delimiters come in every size --- plain, \texttt{\textbackslash left}\ldots\texttt{\textbackslash right}, \texttt{\textbackslash big}, \texttt{\textbackslash Big}, \texttt{\textbackslash bigg}, \texttt{\textbackslash Bigg} --- including the double angle brackets, for which OverLyX added a small macro to this document's preamble:

\begin{equation}
\left\llangle f,g\right\rrangle =\int_{0}^{1}f(x)\,g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x,\qquad\bigl\llangle x\bigr\rrangle \neq\left|x\right|.\label{eq:llangle}
\end{equation}

Your own macros work too. The next paragraph defines one with one argument (Insert\lyxarrow Math\lyxarrow Math macro definition):

\global\long\def\E#1{\mathbb{E}\left[#1\right]}%

From here on $\E{X}$ shows what it stands for (and stays editable, argument included); equation (\ref{eq:macro}) uses it.

\begin{align}
\mathrm{Var}\left[X\right] & =\E{X^{2}}-\E{X}^{2},\label{eq:macro}\\
\mathrm{Cov}\left[X,Y\right] & =\E{XY}-\E{X}\E{Y}.\nonumber 
\end{align}

Press\textsf{ Enter} inside a displayed formula to add a row, \textsf{Tab} to move between cells, and use the matrix button of the math toolbar for something like $\begin{pmatrix}a & b\\ c & d \end{pmatrix}$. The \emph{TeX} button above the document shows its \LaTeX{} source beside it, following the cursor; \emph{PDF} shows the typeset result.

\section{Working together}\label{sec:collab}

\textbf{Sharing.} A project is private to you until you share it --- like a Google Doc. The \emph{Share} button at the top invites people by username or e-mail address as\emph{ viewers} or \emph{ editors}, and can turn on a link that anyone signed in can use. Someone invited by e-mail gets access the moment they sign in with Google.

\textbf{Presence.} Everybody who has the document open appears as a small avatar at the top, and their cursors and selections show in the text in their colour. Click an avatar to jump to where that person is writing.

\textbf{Saving.} There is no Save button: every change goes to the server as you type and is written to the\texttt{ .tex} file a moment later. When the file changes elsewhere --- a git push, an agent, a synced folder --- the document follows. Working offline is fine too: edits are kept in the browser and merged when you are back.

\textbf{Changes, notes and comments.} \textsf{Ctrl+Shift+E} turns on change tracking (or choose \emph{Suggesting} at the top right); each change can be accepted or rejected. Notes are kept in the file but never printed --- this one is a note: %
%% @note
%% A note. It is stored in the file, but never printed.
%% @end
Comments are discussion threads (reply, resolve), and View\lyxarrow Notes \& comments in the margin shows them next to the text: %
%% @comment
%% OverLyX (2026-08-28 09:00):
%%
%% A comment thread. Click it to reply or to mark it resolved.
%% @end
File\lyxarrow Versions keeps named versions and automatic snapshots of the document; any of them can be compared and restored.

\section{Typesetting and exporting}\label{sec:compile}

\textsf{Ctrl+R} typesets the document with\texttt{ pdflatex} (through\texttt{ latexmk}) in the background and shows the PDF beside it; errors and warnings are listed underneath. File\lyxarrow Export downloads the PDF, the \LaTeX{} source or the whole project. Document\lyxarrow Settings changes the class, the preamble, packages and the bibliography style.

\section{Where to go next}\label{sec:next}

\textbf{+ New} on the start page makes a document, slides or a poster, each in a project of its own; \emph{Import from Overleaf} brings your Overleaf projects over. Help\lyxarrow Keyboard shortcuts lists everything, and the source of this editor is at\href{https://github.com/japhba/overlyx}{github.com/japhba/overlyx}.

Used to desktop \LyX ? View\lyxarrow Interface\lyxarrow Classic gives you its menus, toolbars and names, and Settings\lyxarrow Shortcuts takes your key bindings.

Enjoy writing!
