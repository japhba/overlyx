1 · The light

Sunlight is every colour at once

  • White light is a mix of wavelengths from about 380 nm (violet) to 700 nm (red)
  • Air molecules are far smaller than any of them
The prism picture: Newton split it, we just need the wavelengths.

2 · The law

Small particles scatter short waves far more

\[ I \;\propto\; \frac{1}{\lambda^{4}} \]

Rayleigh scattering: halve the wavelength, scatter 16 times as much

The fourth power is the whole story. The curve is plain SVG — an agent can redraw it from data.

3 · The numbers

Blue is scattered about six times more than red

5.9×

\(\left(\frac{700\,\mathrm{nm}}{450\,\mathrm{nm}}\right)^{4} \approx 5.9\)

Violet is scattered even more — but the Sun sends less of it, and our eyes are less sensitive to it.

Pre-empt the violet question.

4 · The sunset

At sunset the light crosses far more air

  • Most blue is scattered away on the long path
  • What reaches you is orange and red
Same law, different path length.

Takeaways

  • Scattering by molecules goes as \(\lambda^{-4}\)
  • Blue light is scattered about six times more than red
  • Long paths at sunset leave the reds
