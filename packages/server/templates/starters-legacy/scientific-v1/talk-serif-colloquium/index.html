I / The question

Does a 70% forecast mean what it says?

Across cases assigned probability 0.7, a calibrated forecast predicts an event frequency of 0.7.

\[\mathbb{E}[Y\mid P=p]=p\]

Calibration is a conditional agreement statement. It does not tell us whether individual cases can be distinguished.

Analytical teaching example 02 / 05

Define P as the forecast and Y as the binary outcome. Calibration is a property of a collection of predictions.

II / The evidence

A reliability diagram reveals the mismatch

Illustrative analytical curves. Dashed: y = p. Solid: y = √p, an underconfident forecast. No empirical samples or confidence intervals are implied.

III / A counterexample

Perfect calibration can carry little information

Suppose the event rate is 20%. Predicting 0.2 for every case is calibrated in a population with that rate.

\[P\equiv 0.2,\qquad \mathbb{E}[Y]=0.2\]

The forecast cannot rank cases by risk. Evaluate resolution or discrimination alongside calibration.
